# 04 Concept

## Objective

Generate and compare distinct creative ideas that answer the approved strategy. Establish the concept before polishing its visual style.

## Required inputs

- A strategy signed off by the authorised human decision-maker.
- The creative criteria, constraints, audience context and relevant research.
- A route template and a documented category-code view where useful.

## Activities

1. Translate each strategy criterion into a creative question.
2. Write at least three different conceptual territories in words, not stylistic versions of one idea, before selecting a visual treatment.
3. For each idea, state the mechanism: what it helps a person notice, understand, remember or do, without claiming an effect not tested.
4. Make rough sketches that test the organising idea; keep them visibly exploratory.
5. Test the idea without visual adjectives: remove words such as bold, warm, minimal or playful and explain what the idea is doing.
6. Only after that test, define a visual language that expresses the idea; style is the expression, not the concept itself.
7. Critique the routes against strategy, distinctiveness, system potential, craft and production risk.

## Research methods

- Use rapid sketching, verbal naming and simple composition studies to expose differences.
- Use category references as evidence of conventions, not as source material to copy.
- Use a route comparison matrix; identify the trade-offs and missing evidence for each direction.

## Deliverables

- At least three routes with distinct idea statements, rationale, rough sketches, risks and strategy links.
- A critique record with evidence labels and a recommended next test, not an approval claim.
- A record of which routes were rejected and why, without erasing the learning.

## Quality criteria

- Routes are different conceptual territories, not stylistic versions of one idea; they differ in underlying idea, not merely colour, typeface or layout.
- Each idea can be described without visual style adjectives.
- The strongest route addresses the agreed criteria and has a credible system path.
- Sketches are rough enough to avoid premature finish bias.
- Cultural meanings and audience responses are not asserted without evidence.

## Common failure modes

- Presenting one polished answer as exploration.
- Changing palette and typography while keeping the same idea and calling it three routes.
- Using references as a substitute for an ownable concept.
- Refining an application before the route idea survives critique.

## Human approval

The studio recommends a route and explains trade-offs. The client chooses a route for development; that choice is a concept decision, not final visual sign-off. The strategy approver must be involved if the route changes the strategy.

## Completion conditions

- Three or more distinct ideas and rough sketches have been critiqued.
- A route is selected or the work returns to strategy with a named question.
- No application polish has hidden an unresolved idea-level problem.
