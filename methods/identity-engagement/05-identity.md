# 05 Identity

## Objective

Translate the selected concept into a coherent set of visual and verbal identity elements, keeping each choice traceable to the idea and approved strategy.

## Required inputs

- An agreed concept route and its decision record.
- Strategy, audience, usage contexts, language requirements and production constraints.
- Relevant font, image, illustration and software licences.

## Activities

1. Define the role of the logo, typography, colour, composition, imagery and verbal foundations in the concept.
2. Develop a responsive logo family and state clear space, minimum size and one-colour behaviour.
3. Select typefaces only after checking licence terms, language and script support, glyph coverage, shaping, accents, numerals, weights and available sizes.
4. Where relevant, test Devanagari and other required scripts with a fluent reviewer; do not assume a Latin font or translation covers them.
5. Define colour values, roles and pairings; check digital text and interface contrast.
6. Specify image and verbal direction with sources, usage rights and unsupported-claim boundaries.
7. Prototype motion only where it strengthens recognition, hierarchy or feedback; describe behaviour before adding effects.

## Research methods

- Use specimen strings for real product names, numerals, punctuation and all required scripts.
- Inspect font licences and source imagery terms before delivery.
- Test logo output at actual intended sizes and in monochrome before detailed finishing.

## Deliverables

- A first [identity system specification](../../templates/identity-systems/identity-system-spec.md).
- Logo family, type rules, colour roles, composition and image direction.
- Verbal identity foundations and sample language, if in agreed scope.
- A rights and open-issues register for selected assets.

## Quality criteria

- Every element has a stated role and relationship to the concept.
- Typography works in the required languages and sizes and is legally usable.
- Logo variants remain recognisable and legible at small sizes.
- Digital colour pairings meet the agreed accessibility baseline or are revised.
- Any motion has a purpose, reduced-motion behaviour and static fallback.

## Common failure modes

- Choosing a typeface by mood while missing a needed script or licence.
- Treating a logo as a complete identity system.
- Using colour alone to encode a required distinction.
- Adding motion because a capability exists rather than because it solves a problem.

## Human approval

The design lead reviews craft and the client confirms factual product language and required scripts. The client’s visual approval is recorded in phase 06 after system testing; review of a component alone does not approve the complete system.

## Completion conditions

- Identity elements have a documented role, usage rule and status.
- Licences and language support are checked or flagged before final selection.
- The system can move into cross-application testing without unresolved core elements.
