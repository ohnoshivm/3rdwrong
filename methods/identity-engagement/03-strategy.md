# 03 Strategy

## Objective

Define the strategic direction and criteria that creative work must answer. This is a decision gate before concept exploration begins.

## Required inputs

- A reviewed diagnosis, source notes and unresolved questions.
- Audience understanding and intended strategic meaning, using the [perception-to-identity map](../perception-to-identity-map.md) without turning it into an outcome claim.
- The approved offer scope, decision roles and practical constraints.
- The strategy-on-a-page template and any requested positioning inputs.

## Activities

1. State the business context and audience using only claims supported by evidence or explicit hypotheses.
2. Define the audience need or tension without turning a single interview into a universal truth.
3. Write the positioning, value proposition and proof boundaries in plain language.
4. Set one coherent strategic direction and the criteria by which concepts will be judged.
5. Identify what the identity must say, enable and avoid; state what is not yet known.
6. Present the strategy separately from visual references so aesthetic preference cannot mask strategic disagreement.

## Research methods

- Use synthesis of primary research, existing materials and linked desk research.
- Use a positioning comparison only where alternatives and source evidence are explicit.
- Use a comprehension check: ask the client to restate the intended audience, choice and proof.

## Deliverables

- A completed [strategy on a page](../../templates/strategies/strategy-on-a-page.md).
- A written direction, audience, context, positioning, promise boundaries and creative criteria.
- A list of hypotheses and questions that concepts may illustrate but cannot resolve alone.
- A recorded strategy approval or a list of required revisions.

## Quality criteria

- The direction is coherent and narrow enough to guide choices.
- Every factual promise has a source or is explicitly an ambition.
- The strategy distinguishes audience need from a chosen expression.
- Criteria can later be checked in words and across applications.
- No visual route is treated as approved by signing the strategy.

## Common failure modes

- Approval of the first mood board is mistaken for strategy approval.
- A proposition promises results the project cannot control.
- Several incompatible audiences are left as if one route can speak equally to all.
- A strategy uses jargon where a plain explanation would expose missing logic.

## Human approval

**Strategy approval is a separate human gate.** The authorised client decision-maker signs the strategy version and date before phase 04 begins. Visual approval occurs later. If strategy changes after this gate, record the change and its effect before continuing.

## Completion conditions

- A named human approver confirms the strategy version.
- Creative criteria are stable enough to guide concept development.
- No concept refinement begins before this approval is recorded.
