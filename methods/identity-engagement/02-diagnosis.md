# 02 Diagnosis

## Objective

Convert discovery evidence into a bounded problem definition. Diagnosis names tensions and causes to investigate without jumping to visual solutions.

## Required inputs

- The discovery synthesis and source inventory.
- The commercial problem and audience evidence boundary from the [perception-to-identity map](../perception-to-identity-map.md), where applicable.
- Approved scope and the decision-maker’s description of the business trigger.
- A category sample if a category-code audit is being used.

## Activities

1. Sort evidence by business, audience, offer, context, current identity and constraint.
2. Mark contradictions, missing voices and claims whose source is weak or out of date.
3. Separate symptoms, such as inconsistent assets, from possible causes, such as unclear positioning; test the link rather than assume it.
4. Compare a documented set of category examples across logo form, type, colour, image, language and layout when category distinction matters.
5. Name category entry codes that support recognition and clichés that add no meaningful reason to choose.
6. Write one problem frame and at least one plausible alternative explanation.

## Research methods

- Use a category-code audit with linked samples; state sample scope and selection bias.
- Use a simple evidence matrix to compare claims, sources, contradictions and confidence.
- Use participant behaviour or existing artefacts to challenge the founder’s initial diagnosis.

## Deliverables

- A diagnosis statement tied to evidence and scope.
- A short tension map: what the business wants, what people encounter and what blocks coherence.
- A category convention and white-space note when sampled evidence supports one.
- Prioritised questions that strategy must resolve.

## Quality criteria

- The diagnosis explains why the issue matters to the agreed business context.
- Interpretations are visibly distinct from documented evidence.
- The sample is large and varied enough for the limited comparison claimed.
- The proposed problem is within the approved identity scope or is flagged as an extension.

## Common failure modes

- Calling an aesthetic preference a diagnosis.
- Claiming an empty white space after reviewing too few or convenience-selected examples.
- Treating a category convention as automatically bad.
- Confusing correlation, a participant quote or a trend with a causal explanation.

## Human approval

The client checks factual accuracy and confirms the problem to address. The studio preserves material uncertainty and proposes alternatives where diagnosis remains weak; client agreement is not evidence that a consumer claim is true.

## Completion conditions

- The problem frame is accepted as the work question, or its unresolved parts are recorded.
- High-impact evidence gaps are resolved, scoped out or explicitly carried forward.
- Strategy can be drafted without inventing audience or commercial facts.
