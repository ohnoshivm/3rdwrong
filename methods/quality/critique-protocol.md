# Critique protocol

A critique tests work against its purpose and evidence. It is not a vote on
taste, a route-selection ritual or a substitute for human approval.
The four modes and written verdicts below are proposed from *Founding
Blueprint v1.0, 22 October 2026 (private archive)*.

## Before the review

- State the phase, decision needed, version and review participants.
- Show the approved strategy and relevant criteria. If strategy is not
  approved, label the review formative and do not imply sign-off.
- Ask the creator to explain the idea in words before presenting polish.
- Separate what is being critiqued from what is already decided.

## Review modes

Use all four modes when evidence is available. A mode can be marked **Not
testable**; do not fill a gap with confidence or an averaged taste score.

| Mode | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| **Concept** | What is the idea in one sentence without visual adjectives? Does it answer the approved strategy? Is it materially different from other territories? What meaning or mechanism is asserted but untested? |
| **Formal** | How do hierarchy, typography, composition, colour, image and craft express the idea? Does the form remain legible and coherent across scale, language and output? Are category conventions used with intention? |
| **Audience** | What interpretation or task should the work support? Which people or behaviours were observed? What is designer interpretation, audience hypothesis or still unknown? |
| **Implementation** | Can the rules produce specified applications? What breaks in digital, print, motion, small-size or specialist production? Are files, rights, accessibility and fallback behaviour ready? |

Across those modes, assess fit with strategy, strength of the underlying idea,
distinctiveness against a sourced sample, system repeatability and craft under
the conditions tested. Distinguish visual style from idea: the idea must
survive a description without visual adjectives.

## Evidence labels

Use these exact labels for each material claim:

- **Documented** — stated in a cited primary or credible secondary source.
- **Interpretation** — our reasoned reading of documented material.
- **Hypothesis** — plausible but untested; state how it could be tested.
- **Unknown** — not publicly documented; do not fill the gap.

A fictional input may be Documented within that fiction only. It is not
external audience evidence. Put a source beside the claim, and mark unsupported
market or consumer claims as Hypothesis or Unknown.

## Severity

- **Blocker:** breaks approved strategy, creates a rights or safety issue, or
  makes a core application unusable. Stop approval and resolve or formally
  change scope.
- **Major:** weakens a key criterion or system rule across more than one use.
  Resolve before visual sign-off, or record a named human exception.
- **Minor:** local craft issue with limited effect. Fix in the current round or
  log the accepted exception and owner.
- **Observation:** useful possibility or question, not a defect. Do not treat
  it as required work without a reason.

For each finding, record the lens, evidence label and source, severity,
consequence, suggested next check and owner. Distinguish a finding from a
solution suggestion. End with open questions and a recommendation; the human
approver records the actual decision.

## Verdict and next action

Choose one written verdict, not a numeric score or average:

- **Strong enough to advance**
- **Promising but structurally unresolved**
- **Technically sound but conceptually weak**
- **Distinctive but commercially inappropriate**
- **Rejected, with explicit reasons**

The verdict summarises evidence and does not approve work. Every critique ends
with a concrete next design action, its owner and the evidence needed to
complete it. If no action can be proposed responsibly, name the missing input
that must be supplied first.
