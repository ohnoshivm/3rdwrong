# Creative guardrails

These proposed rules help research and tools become original, useful work.
They are general practice guidance derived from *Founding Blueprint v1.0, 22
October 2026 (private archive)*, not claims about any individual's ability.

## Rules for practice

1. **Research must produce an original application.** After a substantial
   study, make an exercise, method revision or project decision that applies
   one transferable principle. Record what was changed and what remained
   specific to the source.
2. **Move from concept to coherent static identity to advanced expression.**
   Prove the idea and its static system before adding generative behaviour,
   motion or complex interaction. A technical layer must solve a design need.
3. **Build typography competence before advanced 3D.** Practise hierarchy,
   spacing, script coverage, legibility and composition in ordinary work
   before taking on specialist production complexity.
4. **Widen the reference field.** Use architecture, literature, cinema, local
   art, objects, industrial design, scientific visualisation, music and
   cultural history alongside professional identity work. Record source and
   context; don't turn an image library into a substitute for interpretation.
5. **Watch for derivative voice.** Oversized grotesk type, saturated colour,
   algorithmic graphics and editorial layouts can be useful, but become
   derivative when borrowed as a studio costume without a project-specific
   reason.
6. **Seek informed critique.** When stakes or technical demands exceed the
   team's judgement, commission critique from experienced practitioners and
   record what changed. A paid opinion is input, not automatic approval.
7. **Delay proprietary tools until a repeated client need exposes a gap.**
   Prefer existing tools and simple documented methods until the same
   bottleneck recurs and a tool can remove it responsibly.
8. **Choose markets partly by buying behaviour.** Creative interest is not
   enough. Investigate triggers, budget authority, procurement, alternative
   providers and access to decision-makers.

## Applying a guardrail

For each project, state the relevant rule, the decision it affects and the
evidence that would change the choice. These guardrails do not require a
specific aesthetic or software stack. Exceptions should be documented with
their project context and consequences; an exception is not a universal
precedent.
