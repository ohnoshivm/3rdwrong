# creative-direction

## Purpose

Translate signed strategy into meaningful identity ideas. Distinguish a creative idea from visual style and show route trade-offs before detailed design.

## When to use

Use after the phase 03 strategy gate, when the signed version, criteria and constraints are available. Use for early identity exploration, not final production artwork.

## When not to use

Do not use when strategy is missing, provisional or disputed. Do not start by choosing a mood, style or logo reference. Do not present AI output as original, culturally valid, rights-cleared or approved without human review.

## Inputs

- Signed strategy with approver, date and version.
- Audience, positioning, proof boundaries and creative criteria.
- Relevant category audit and its source limitations.
- Language, channel, production, accessibility and rights constraints.
- Desired number of routes; default to at least three distinct ideas.

## Dependencies

Read [phase 03 Strategy](../../methods/identity-engagement/03-strategy.md), [phase 04 Concept](../../methods/identity-engagement/04-concept.md), the [concept route template](../../templates/identity-systems/concept-route.md) and [critique protocol](../../methods/quality/critique-protocol.md). No external paid tools are required; rough sketches may be described or supplied by the designer.

## Procedure

1. Verify human strategy sign-off, date and version. If any are missing, stop and ask; do not infer approval from a polished board or informal email.
2. Extract the intended audience, positioning, proof boundaries, constraints and creative criteria; link the exact source.
3. Convert each criterion into a creative question. Separate functional constraints from aesthetic preferences.
4. Develop at least three ideas with different organising logic. Do not create variants by changing only typeface, palette, texture or composition.
5. Write each idea in one sentence without visual adjectives. Explain what it helps a person notice, understand, remember or do, without claiming an untested effect.
6. Link rationale to strategy and evidence. Label interpretations and hypotheses; do not claim memory, recognition or preference without a test.
7. Describe a rough sketch that exposes the organising idea before application polish.
8. Only then define visual expression: logo behaviour, type roles, colour logic, composition and image direction. Explain how each expresses the idea.
   Keep at least one sketch low-fidelity so idea-level critique is not biased by finish.
   Check that the chosen metaphors do not depend on an unsupported cultural association.
9. Record system potential, distinctiveness, language, accessibility, licensing, production risks and disconfirming evidence.
10. Recommend routes for exploration with trade-offs. State that selection for development is not visual sign-off.

## Evidence requirements

Link criteria to the signed strategy. Use **Documented**, **Interpretation**, **Hypothesis** and **Unknown** exactly. Route reasoning is usually interpretation; audience effect is a hypothesis until tested. Record reference sources and do not reproduce distinctive expression. Mark material AI assistance and require human originality and rights review.

## Output

Return one instance per route, followed by a comparison.

```markdown
# Concept route: {{neutral route name}}

**Status:** proposed exploration
**Strategy version:** {{link, approver, date}}

## Idea in one sentence
{{idea without visual adjectives}}

## Strategy link
- {{criterion}} — {{evidence label and source}}

## Rationale
{{how idea answers strategy; label inference}}

## Rough sketch
{{description or link; expose idea before polish}}

## Visual expression
- Logo behaviour: {{role}}
- Typography: {{role and script checks}}
- Colour: {{role, not only palette}}
- Composition and imagery: {{rules}}

## System potential and risks
{{applications, access, language, production, rights, unknowns}}

## Disconfirming test
{{observation that would weaken this route}}

## Recommendation
{{develop, revise or reject; no approval claim}}
```

Then add a route comparison with idea distinction, strategy fit, risk and next test for each route.

## Quality checks

- Strategy approval is verified before routes are created.
- At least three materially different ideas are present.
- Each idea passes the no-visual-adjectives test.
- Rough sketches precede application polish.
- Distinctiveness has a category evidence link or stays a hypothesis.
- Each route has a disconfirming test and practical risks.
- Recommendation and human approval remain separate.
- Routes have comparable detail so one is not favoured by polish alone.
- Any source reference is linked and described as context, never as permission to copy.

- Each route names a decision that would change if its idea were removed.
- No route claims commercial impact outside the studio’s control.

## Failure conditions

Stop and ask for a signed strategy if it is absent, disputed or missing approval details. Stop if incompatible criteria need a human decision. Ask for missing language, rights or production constraints when they could invalidate a route. Do not fill gaps with invented audience meaning.

## Human review

A creative lead checks originality, context, strategy fit and system potential. The client selects a route for development; the authorised human records that decision. Final visual approval follows system testing. This skill never marks a route approved.

## Example

**Fictional example.** For a synthetic refill shop strategy, one idea is “make each return complete a loop between use and refill”. A circular motif is one possible expression, not the idea itself. A second route needs a different organising logic, not only a new colour. No response or recognition claim is made without testing.
