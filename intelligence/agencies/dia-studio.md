# DIA — kinetic identity and programmable variation

**Interpretation** — These projects support studying kinetic and programmable identity practice. They do
not justify inventing a fixed internal workflow, current team structure or universal software stack.

## Business or creative problem

**Interpretation** — The agency-level question is how motion can shape an identity concept rather than
decorate a completed static mark. The three projects provide different kinds of evidence and should not
be flattened into one technique.

**Unknown** — The reviewed pages do not provide comparable full client briefs, decision criteria or

## Strategic or conceptual insight

**Interpretation** — The shared question is behavioural authorship: what relationship should persist as
the form changes? Typography, semantic movement and scheduled variation offer different answers.

**Interpretation** — Programmable output is not the same as autonomous creative judgment. Defining
inputs, limits and selection criteria remains part of designing the identity.

## Visual and technical decisions

### Documented rule boundary

**Documented** — Continuous phrase-led loops are described for
[Nike](https://dia.tv/project/nike-basketball/); scheduled generative content is described for
[Adidas](https://dia.tv/project/adidas-flagship-london/).

**Unknown** — This does not establish that every DIA animation is generated, that all tools are delivered
to clients, or that one codebase powers all projects.

### Interpretation across projects

**Interpretation** — For a typographic behavioural system, assess whether the message remains readable
through transformation. A movement that visually embodies a phrase can still fail if its key information
cannot be recovered.

**Interpretation** — For a continuous loop, the transition across the boundary matters as much as any
single impressive frame. This makes temporal continuity a design constraint rather than post-production
polish.

**Interpretation** — For a generator, judge a sample of outputs rather than the best selected result. An
expressive tool could produce a high proportion of unusable work unless its constraints are sufficiently
clear.

## Why these decisions may work

**Interpretation** — A repeated behaviour could supply continuity even when no frame is identical. This
creates a different basis for coherence from repeatedly placing a fixed mark over new footage.

**Interpretation** — Semantic movement could make an expression feel purposeful; equally, it might demand
too much attention for routine product information. Fit depends on the task and the audience, not on
motion sophistication alone.

**Hypothesis** — A behaviour derived from a real product action could help viewers connect a message and
an identity. Compare it with an equally polished unrelated movement, measuring message comprehension and
attribution separately.

## Transferable principles

**Interpretation** — Start with a behavioural proposition and prototype it before creating a long list of
deliverables. Determine whether the idea survives a static frame and difficult copy.

**Interpretation** — Distinguish a fixed animation, a reusable template and a live generator in the
scope. They impose different operating and maintenance requirements; do not sell one while delivering
another.

**Interpretation** — Compare the constrained transformation in the [Spotify
study](../projects/collins-spotify.md). Repeatable operations can support identity, but neither custom
software nor arbitrary variation is required in every case.

## What not to copy

**Interpretation** — Do not imitate stretched lettering, looping typographic tricks or a portfolio's
kinetic signature as a shortcut to originality. Derive the behaviour from the new brand's own meaning and
practical requirements.

**Interpretation** — Do not describe AI output as DIA's process. These sources document selected projects
and tools, not a general claim about AI authorship.

**Interpretation** — Do not assume type licences cover editable client generators, every script or every
distribution channel. Verify the actual licence and intended handover before selecting a type system.

## Application to 3RDWRONG

**Hypothesis** — For a founder-led Indian consumer brand, test one behaviour tied to a meaningful product
action: opening, unfolding or combining, only where that action actually belongs to the product. Avoid
inventing a story to justify a visual trick.

**Hypothesis** — Build a small motion prototype and a static alternative using real words and required
scripts. Let the intended operator change a product name and prepare a new output; record where the rule
breaks.

**Interpretation** — International technology or sports brands do not establish an
appropriate budget or expressive intensity for a smaller client. A carefully authored loop may be enough;
bespoke software must earn its place.

**Hypothesis** — For any generator proposal, budget a separate experiment to test useful-output rate,
input rights, maintenance responsibility and export requirements. Do not imply that this extension is
automatically included in an identity engagement.

## Exercise

**Hypothesis** — Proposed exercise: define one original product-related behaviour, then make a static
identity and a short loop using it. Separately specify a hypothetical content scheduler; name the
boundaries between identity design, motion assets, content capture and generative CMS.

**Hypothesis** — Produce an operator brief, an exception example and a responsibility map suitable for
specialist collaboration. Compare a pre-rendered template with a generator proposal; select the smaller
solution unless evidence from repeated use warrants more complexity.
