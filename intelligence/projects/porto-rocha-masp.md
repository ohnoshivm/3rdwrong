## Business or creative problem

**Documented** — The studio describes an identity for MASP's expansion, linking institutional continuity
with a new building and physical and digital communication. [Project
page](https://www.portorocha.com/masp).

**Interpretation** — The useful problem is how to make an expanded organisation legible as one entity
while retaining existing associations. Novelty alone would not answer that problem.

## Strategic or conceptual insight

**Interpretation** — The institution's environment can supply a logic rather than a decorative motif. The
stronger transfer is to examine how a client's real structure might organise communication, not to
stylise an unrelated building.

**Interpretation** — Continuity is a design decision. An audit of what must remain recognisable should
precede the decision about how much visual change to introduce.

## Visual and technical decisions

### Documented versus reconstructable

**Documented** — The published account explicitly links architectural form, wordmark revision and modular
composition. [Project page](https://www.portorocha.com/masp).

**Unknown** — Grid dimensions, responsive breakpoints, numerical motion curves and full font licensing
terms are not supplied in the reviewed account.

### Interpretation of system relations

**Interpretation** — A block-based vocabulary could unite large signs and small information modules
because both can express the same spatial logic. That is a compositional proposition to test, not a claim
that the actual website meets every accessibility requirement.

**Interpretation** — A two-part symbol could communicate institutional expansion more directly than a
wholly new unrelated icon. Whether visitors understand that reference without explanation remains a
separate research question.

**Interpretation** — A grid's value lies in what it can accommodate. It should be assessed with competing
information priorities, awkward content lengths and missing imagery, not only balanced exhibition
posters.

## Why these decisions may work

**Interpretation** — Linking old and new elements can give designers a reason for consistency without
freezing the institution in a former expression. The design might support change by making the underlying
continuity visible.

**Interpretation** — Architecture can also become an inward-looking reference. An audience unfamiliar
with the building may recognise a graphic family without understanding its conceptual origin; this would
not necessarily be a failure.

**Hypothesis** — A retained cue could reduce mistaken attribution during a redesign. Test the old, wholly
new and selectively retained alternatives with comparable exposure and collect wrong-brand responses as
well as correct ones.

## Transferable principles

**Interpretation** — Audit equity before editing form. A visually awkward old element may still be worth
testing before removal; designer preference alone cannot establish its market value.

**Interpretation** — Make a core logic survive changes in content density. The [Whitney
study](experimental-jetset-whitney.md) offers a different approach to the same broad problem of content
and institutional voice sharing a surface.

**Interpretation** — Separate the useful constraint from its source material. A consumer business might
derive a rule from product use or assortment structure rather than a museum's architecture.

## What not to copy

**Interpretation** — Do not reproduce MASP's block lettering, red-and-black building pair, modernist
references or exhibition compositions. A new client's story must earn its own formal vocabulary.

**Interpretation** — Do not promise a website, signage programme or sonic identity merely because they
appear in this reference. Agree the commissioned identity scope and any separately delivered extensions
first.

## Application to 3RDWRONG

**Hypothesis** — For a founder-led Indian consumer business considering a refresh, inventory its current
name, pack silhouette, recurring colour, verbal cue and image treatment. Test candidate equity before
proposing elimination.

**Hypothesis** — Build a compact grammar using actual product information. Demonstrate it on one product
label concept, one retailer sheet and one launch image; packaging engineering remains a separate
question.

**Interpretation** — A museum's physical landmark and established institutional role are not available to
every emerging brand. Do not invent a heritage narrative or imply that an architectural metaphor supplies
market recognition.

**Hypothesis** — If the project requires Indian scripts, prototype real bilingual text in the intended
sizes. Reject a system whose hierarchy only works with short Latin headlines; the reference's font choice
provides no evidence of suitability for those scripts.

## Exercise

**Hypothesis** — Proposed exercise, adapted from Founding Blueprint v1.0, 22 October 2026 ([founding source archive](../../studio/archive/founding-blueprint-v1.0.md)): reposition a hypothetical independent cultural institution in India. Select a real regional
design history and verify it through local archives, institutions and credited practitioners before
extracting any formal principles.

**Hypothesis** — Label the institution fictional and its strategic claims hypothetical. Develop an
original static system for local and international audiences, with real required scripts and three
contrasting applications. Explain what the historical evidence permits and where cultural interpretation
remains uncertain; do not fabricate heritage or borrow MASP forms.
