# 3RDWRONGS — Initial Ideal Customer Profile

Version 0.1 | Primary ICP hypothesis

> Founder-authored source document. Every element here is an unvalidated hypothesis
> ([D-0004](decisions/0004-initial-ideal-customer-profile.md), [D-0005](decisions/0005-pricing-hypothesis.md)).
> Do not quote it externally as market fact.

## Primary market

Founder-led, emerging premium consumer and lifestyle brands that have demonstrated commercial traction and are entering a meaningful growth or repositioning stage.

These are prospecting priorities, not permanent industry restrictions.

## Business characteristics

- **Stage:** Operating business with products, customers, and some evidence of demand.
- **Size:** Typically a small or growing team, approximately 5–50 employees.
- **Geography:** India initially, especially brands accessible through direct founder relationships. International clients remain an opportunity.
- **Commercial position:** Premium or aspirational, with reasons to invest in perceived quality and distinction.
- **Budget:** Tentative ability to invest ₹1.5–3.5 lakh in a focused identity project, subject to validation.
- **Decision-maker:** Founder, co-founder, brand head, or marketing leader with meaningful creative authority.

## The problems they experience

Their existing identity no longer reflects the quality, ambition, or direction of the business.

They may struggle with inconsistent visual expression, similarity to competitors, an underdeveloped brand personality, fragmented digital and physical applications, or an identity that cannot support their next stage of growth.

We should confirm these problems through discovery rather than assume every prospect experiences them.

## High-intent buying triggers

1. Preparing for a significant launch, expansion, or repositioning.
2. Moving from an informal founder-created identity to a professional brand system.
3. Entering a more competitive or premium market.
4. Expanding into new product categories or distribution channels.
5. Experiencing operational problems caused by inconsistent branding.

## What they are buying

Not simply a logo, colour palette, or collection of mockups.

They are commissioning a distinctive, coherent identity that communicates their intended position, supports their customer experience, and provides usable rules for future communication.

Our core engagement is **The 3RDWRONGS Identity System** ([offer specification](identity-system-offer.md)).

## Qualification criteria

**Strong fit:** A genuine business problem, clear decision-maker, realistic budget, willingness to participate in discovery, and openness to original creative thinking.

**Weak fit:** Businesses primarily seeking cheap logo production, unlimited design revisions, trend replication, or services outside our core specialisation.

**Particularly valuable:** Clients who will allow the work to be publicly documented and provide credible evidence of its practical implementation.

## Secondary ICP

Early-stage creative technology and software businesses preparing for launch or repositioning, especially when their products would benefit from digital-native or living identity systems.

These projects are attractive for portfolio development but should not dilute the primary market-testing effort.

## Recommended discovery focus (proposed)

The positioning is coherent, but the target market is still broader than our initial proof. Beauty, fashion, homeware, and technology all have different consumer behaviour, competition, buying cycles, and brand requirements.

Begin discovery with emerging beauty and personal-care brands, keeping fashion and lifestyle brands as secondary prospects. The reason is not an assumption that beauty offers the largest market; it is that packaging, perception, recognition, premium positioning, and visual consistency are closely connected to how these businesses compete. We still need evidence that they can afford our services. Recorded as [D-0010](decisions/0010-beauty-personal-care-discovery-focus.md).

## What we must validate

Before declaring this our permanent ICP, interview at least 15–20 qualified buyers and investigate:

- Whether identity problems are urgent enough to justify paid work.
- Which events actually trigger buying decisions.
- Typical budgets and procurement processes.
- Whether clients value motion and adaptive identity capabilities.
- Who influences and approves creative work.
- Which competing providers prospects consider.
- What evidence would make them trust a newly established studio.

**Success means identifying a repeatable category of clients who both value our work and have the ability and willingness to pay for it.**

## How to record validation evidence

Choose a non-public store before collecting any interview data; personal and identifying notes stay there
(see [publication rules](../docs/public-private-boundary.md)). Use anonymised interview ids, record consent and its scope,
and remove identifying combinations. Only anonymised, aggregated findings —
for example "9 of 16 interviewed founders named a launch as the trigger" — come back here, with the date and
sample size, through a pull request that updates this file and, where the conclusion changes, a decision record.
