# Creative philosophy

The eight principles below are **approved verbatim** operating philosophy.
Their practical interpretations and examples are **proposed**. Examples are
fictional illustrations, not client stories.

## Meaning before appearance

**Approved principle:** Meaning before appearance.

**Proposed meaning:** State what the identity must communicate and to whom
before choosing a look. A reference image can describe a mood, but cannot
substitute for a decision about meaning.

**Decision effect:** Require a written idea and strategy link before polishing
marks or choosing palettes.

**Strong illustration:** For a fictional refillable soap company, the route
idea is “make returning a container feel like completing a useful cycle”. The
team tests that idea in the refill label, return instruction and shop sign
before refining a motif.

**Weak illustration:** Choosing sage green and a leaf because the company sells
soap, then writing a sustainability story to justify the colour.

## Distinctiveness before fashionable conformity

**Approved principle:** Distinctiveness before fashionable conformity.

**Proposed meaning:** Learn category conventions, preserve the codes people
need to recognise the offer, and challenge conventions that do not help this
brand.

**Decision effect:** Compare routes against a coded category sample, not a
mood-board impression.

**Strong illustration:** A fictional skin-care brand keeps legible product
hierarchy and ingredient information while rejecting the category's repeated
apothecary border after finding no strategic reason for it.

**Weak illustration:** Giving every product an ironic grotesk wordmark because
that treatment is visible in current design feeds.

## Systems before isolated artefacts

**Approved principle:** Systems before isolated artefacts.

**Proposed meaning:** Define rules and relationships that generate coherent
assets, not a hero logo that breaks when a team makes the next item.

**Decision effect:** Test hierarchy, spacing, colour roles and image rules in
multiple agreed applications before approval.

**Strong illustration:** A fictional spice company uses a stable typographic
hierarchy and a changing origin stamp; a new product remains recognisable
without copying a single front label.

**Weak illustration:** Delivering one striking launch poster with no rule for
product names, small labels or retailer listings.

## Expression with restraint

**Approved principle:** Expression with restraint.

**Proposed meaning:** Give the strongest idea enough room to register. Remove
decoration that competes with it or adds production burden without meaning.

**Decision effect:** For each expressive device, state its role and where it
should stop.

**Strong illustration:** A fictional tea identity uses a single hand-drawn
line to connect harvest and steeping; instructions remain calm and readable.

**Weak illustration:** Putting foil, embossing, animation, grain, hand lettering
and a moving background on every touchpoint because each looks premium alone.

## Originality through informed experimentation

**Approved principle:** Originality through informed experimentation.

**Proposed meaning:** Try unfamiliar formal choices after understanding the
brief, context and production limits; retain what survives useful tests.

**Decision effect:** Record hypothesis, controlled change, observation and
adopt/iterate/drop decision for experiments.

**Strong illustration:** A fictional olive-oil label tests a punched aperture
against a flat illustration, then checks legibility, paper waste and shelf
recognition at actual scale.

**Weak illustration:** Generating dozens of images and selecting the strangest
one without checking audience, source rights or production.

## Human creative judgment leads; AI expands capability

**Approved principle:** Human creative judgment leads; AI expands capability.

**Proposed meaning:** People choose the problem, interpret evidence, direct
experiments and take responsibility for a recommendation. AI can help explore,
organise or inspect; its output is not approval or evidence by itself.

**Decision effect:** Record where AI materially shaped work, check facts and
rights, and have a person select or reject the result.

**Strong illustration:** An AI-assisted list of visual metaphors becomes a
set of rough alternatives; the designer rejects unsupported cultural
associations after research and writes a human-led route rationale.

**Weak illustration:** Treating an image model's culturally coded output as
proof that a symbol has a particular local meaning.

## Evidence and honest critique matter more than impressive terminology

**Approved principle:** Evidence and honest critique matter more than
impressive terminology.

**Proposed meaning:** Make a claim inspectable. Identify what is sourced, what
is interpreted, what is assumed and what remains unknown.

**Decision effect:** Use the evidence labels in the [critique protocol](../methods/quality/critique-protocol.md)
and record sources next to factual claims.

**Strong illustration:** A fictional founder interview supports a documented
statement about that participant; the team labels a broader consumer reaction
as hypothesis until it is tested.

**Weak illustration:** Calling a route “neuro-optimised” without a specific
study, method or measured evidence.

## Exceptional execution matters more than the number of tools installed

**Approved principle:** Exceptional execution matters more than the number of
tools installed.

**Proposed meaning:** Choose tools for the production need and finish the work
well. Software count is not a proxy for judgement or craft.

**Decision effect:** Define the output and quality check first; add a tool only
when it materially improves control, speed or reliability.

**Strong illustration:** A designer checks a logo in a vector editor, tests
print output with the chosen vendor and uses a simple motion prototype only
after static rules are sound.

**Weak illustration:** Building an unnecessary 3D pipeline for a wordmark that
will be delivered as a two-colour print mark.
