# Operating rhythm

**Proposed.** The studio's fixed meetings with itself: what happens daily,
weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly, and what each produces. Designed for a
founder-led studio with collaborators and AI agents; every ritual works with
one person.

## Purpose

Protect craft time while keeping sales, cash and learning from slipping.
Decisions happen at a known time with known inputs, so nothing waits on
memory or mood.

## When it applies

Always. When a client deadline collides with a ritual, shorten the ritual;
do not skip two in a row.

## Roles

Operations — planning-workflow owns the calendar and templates. The
**founder** chairs every review. Collaborators join only the parts about
their work. Production brings delivery status; Sales brings pipeline;
Operations — finance-legal brings cash.

## Inputs

The project boards (Delivery engine), the pipeline (Growth engine), the vault
finance records, the [scorecard](scorecard.md), the [review queue](review-queue.md).

## Steps

### Daily — focus (10 minutes, morning)

1. Pick the one outcome that matters most today and block the craft time for
   it, before email.
2. Check: any client waiting on the studio for more than one working day?
   Any invoice due or overdue? Answer those first.
3. Close the day with a two-line log: what moved, what is blocked.

### Monday — plan (30 minutes)

1. Read Friday's review and its three outcomes for the week.
2. Lay out the week: client checkpoints, proposal deadlines, collaborator
   handoffs, invoices to raise.
3. Book craft blocks first, then sales and admin around them.
4. Send collaborators their week's briefs and confirm capacity.

### Friday — review (30 minutes)

Render the [weekly-review bundle](../templates/bundles/weekly-review/) into
the vault and fill it in order: last week's commitments, delivery health,
pipeline, cash, studio, next week's three outcomes. Do the weekly
bookkeeping from [finance](finance/README.md). Clear one review-queue session
if time allows.

### Monthly — business review (90 minutes, first week)

1. Close the books and complete the
   [finance-review bundle](../templates/bundles/finance-review/).
2. Update the [scorecard](scorecard.md): money metrics in the vault,
   non-money metrics where agreed.
3. Review every win/loss entry from the month ([proposals](proposals.md)).
4. Review project health and collaborator performance.
5. Decide: up to three changes for next month (price, process, offer,
   focus). Record them as decisions or review-queue items.

### Quarterly — strategy and offer review (half day)

1. Are the offers selling at target margin? Compare estimated and actual
   hours by stage; adjust the estimation guide and rates.
2. Review positioning and ICP evidence with Growth: which segments convert,
   at which option.
3. Review the review queue's oldest items and the roadmap.
4. Review collaborator bench: gaps, rates, quality.
5. Set the next quarter's three priorities and stop at least one activity.
6. Check tax: advance tax, GST returns, LUT validity, TDS reconciliation.

### Annual — reset (one day, at financial-year end)

1. Read the year's scorecards, decisions and retrospectives.
2. Revisit positioning, offers, pricing, rates, target margin and reserve
   target.
3. Close the accounts with the CA; renew the LUT; renew licences and tools
   only if used.
4. Retire stale documents; refresh the review queue.
5. Write the next year's three goals and the evidence that would show them
   met.

## Outputs

Weekly reviews and monthly finance reviews in the vault; decisions and
review-queue items in the repository; updated scorecard.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Friday review written every week; three outcomes set.
- [ ] Monthly review held in the first week with closed books.
- [ ] Quarterly review produces three priorities and one stopped activity.
- [ ] Every review leaves dated decisions, not only notes.

## Metrics

Reviews held on schedule; share of weekly outcomes achieved; founder hours by
category (client, sales, studio, learning) from the weekly review.

## Failure modes

- Reviews that become status reports with no decisions.
- Skipping reviews in busy weeks, which is when they matter most.
- Monday plans that ignore client dependencies.
- Too many priorities; nothing gets the craft time.
