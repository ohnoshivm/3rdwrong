# Client acquisition

**Status: proposed.** These are public-safe business-development practices,
not a proven acquisition system. Source direction: *Founding Blueprint v1.0,
22 October 2026 (private archive)*.

## Operating principle

Use founder-led, targeted business development. Choose a small set of
prospects whose business context and identity needs can be understood; do not
send a generic capability blast or imply that a prospect has a problem the
studio has not investigated.

## Prospect categories to test

- Founder-led emerging beauty and personal-care brands with a visible launch,
  repositioning or consistency challenge.
- Select fashion and lifestyle businesses as secondary discovery prospects.
- Early-stage creative-technology and software businesses as a secondary
  portfolio and learning segment; do not let it dilute primary-market testing.
- Cultural and independent hospitality businesses as an alternative segment
  to evaluate through access and purchase behaviour.
- Partner agencies that lack an identity capability for a defined project,
  where responsibilities and client relationships can be made clear.

These are prospect categories to test, not claims of market size or current
fit. Prioritise access to the actual decision-maker and record response and
closing behaviour.

## Outreach standard

1. Research the prospect using public, relevant sources and retain links and
   access dates.
2. State why the work may be relevant to the prospect's actual context. Mark
   any interpretation as such and avoid unsupported diagnosis.
3. Point to one relevant piece of evidence or method, not an invented result.
4. Ask for a specific next conversation or referral with a clear reason.
5. Record response, objection, timing, alternative provider and next step in
   the private business-development log.
6. Respect non-response and opt-outs; do not continue an unwanted sequence.

Never send unsolicited speculative rebrands framed as criticism of a
prospect's existing identity. A useful conversation starts with curiosity and
evidence, not a public teardown or an unsolicited solution.

## Objections to investigate

The following are **Hypotheses**, not statements heard from buyers. Record the
prospect's actual words and context before adapting a response.

| Possible objection | Honest response and next question |
| --- | --- |
| “We already have a designer or agency.” | If the current support solves the problem, there is no reason to replace it. Ask whether a specific identity decision or capability remains uncovered. |
| “We only need a logo.” | A logo-only request may not need a full system. Ask what decision or application is failing; offer the Sprint only if a strategic direction would help. |
| “We need proof before hiring a new studio.” | Acknowledge the absence of client outcomes. Show the method and clearly labelled self-initiated work; do not imply that these demonstrate client results. |
| “We can generate the assets with AI.” | Agree that production is increasingly accessible. Explain that the offer focuses on the problem, original concept, coherent rules and real-use checks; make no performance claim. |
| “The full engagement is too much for this decision.” | Do not reduce the full system until careful work becomes impossible. Check whether a bounded Sprint can answer the immediate question, or recommend no engagement. |

Use these only as listening prompts. Revise or remove them when actual
conversation evidence contradicts them.

## Learn from acquisition

For each qualified opportunity, log the source, segment, trigger, decision
role, perceived problem, scope discussed, objections, outcome and reason for
win or loss. Keep prospect identities, notes and private commercial material
out of the public repository. Review the aggregated evidence when validating
the [initial ICP](../studio/initial-icp.md), the
[Brand Direction Sprint](../studio/brand-direction-sprint.md) and the
[Identity System](../studio/identity-system-offer.md).
