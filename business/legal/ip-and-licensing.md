# IP and licensing guide

**Template, not legal advice — have a qualified lawyer in India review before use.**

**Proposed.** What the studio owns, what it transfers, what it licenses and
what it must disclose. Applies to every deliverable from L3 Propose to L9
Close-out.

## Purpose

Make sure the client receives the rights they paid for, the studio keeps the
rights it needs to keep working, and nobody ships material the studio has no
right to use.

## When it applies

Writing the SOW (what is assigned or licensed), choosing any third-party
asset during delivery, preparing handover, and before any publication.

## Roles

Design and Engineering leads record every third-party asset as it enters a
project. Operations — finance-legal checks licences before handover. The
**founder** approves the rights section of each SOW and every publication.

## Inputs

The SOW deliverable list, the project's third-party asset register (vault),
licence texts, the collaborator agreements for everyone who worked on the
project.

## Steps

### 1. Assignment or licence

| Approach | Use when | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Assignment** of copyright | Identity marks, guidelines, bespoke artwork, site design and bespoke code the client must own outright | Must be in writing, signed, and state rights, territory and duration (Copyright Act, 1957, section 19); the MSA assigns on full payment |
| **Exclusive licence** | The studio needs to keep ownership (for example a generative system or tool) but the client needs exclusivity in its field | Define field, territory, term and modification rights |
| **Non-exclusive licence** | Studio pre-existing material: code libraries, templates, the house engine, methods | Perpetual and royalty-free inside the deliverable; no resale as a standalone product |

Record in the SOW which deliverables are assigned and which are licensed.
The studio can only assign what it owns: every collaborator must have signed
the [collaborator agreement](collaborator-agreement.md) first.

### 2. Fonts

- Fonts are software licensed per use: desktop, web, app embedding, digital
  advertising, broadcast and logo use often need different licences.
- The client buys and holds the licences needed for ongoing use, in its own
  name. The studio's own licences do not transfer.
- Before handover, list each typeface, foundry, licence type and licensee.
  Never deliver font files the client is not licensed for.
- Open-licence fonts (for example under the SIL Open Font License) can be
  used and shipped subject to their terms; keep the licence file.
- A wordmark drawn from a commercial typeface: check the foundry's terms
  for logo use; custom lettering avoids the question.

### 3. Stock and third-party content

- Check licence scope: media, print runs, territory, term, sensitive-use
  limits, model and property releases.
- Do not build a logo or mark on stock elements: the client cannot get
  exclusive rights to them.
- Buy licences in the client's name where the platform allows; otherwise
  record the licence and its transferability.

### 4. AI-generated assets

- **Unknown** — Indian law does not yet settle who owns, or whether copyright
  subsists in, material generated by AI tools. The Copyright Act's definition
  of the author of a computer-generated work (section 2(d)(vi)) has not been
  tested on generative AI. In the United States, the Copyright Office has
  stated that copyright requires human authorship ([Copyright and AI](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/)).
- Consequence: an AI-generated element may not be ownable or assignable. Do
  not use AI-generated material in a mark, logo or any element the client
  must own exclusively.
- Check each tool's terms on output ownership, commercial use, indemnity and
  training on inputs. Never enter client confidential material or personal
  data into a tool that may retain or train on it.
- Disclose to the client, in the handover asset register, every deliverable
  that contains AI-generated material, the tool used and how it was edited.
  The MSA (clause 9) carries this duty.

### 5. Open source in deliverables

- List every open-source dependency and its licence in the handover.
- Permissive licences (MIT, BSD, Apache 2.0) need notices kept; Apache 2.0
  also carries patent terms.
- Copyleft licences (GPL, AGPL, LGPL in some uses) can require releasing
  source code of the combined work. Do not include them in client
  deliverables without the client's written agreement.
- Studio-authored code released as open source (Labs, showcase) is licensed
  under the repository's licences; client code is never published without
  written permission.

### 6. Portfolio and anonymised publication ([D-0021](../../studio/decisions/0021-client-work-public-anonymised.md))

- Default: client work enters this public repository only anonymised —
  codename, no client name, people, contacts, money, client files or
  identifying combinations — after the [D-0021](../../studio/decisions/0021-client-work-public-anonymised.md) clearance check run by the
  [Delivery engine](../../methods/lifecycle.md).
- Named portfolio use, award entries and case studies follow MSA clause 7
  ([D-0023](../../studio/decisions/0023-commercial-and-delivery-defaults.md)): only after public launch, with client-approved case-study wording,
  unless the client opted out as confidential in the contract.
- Never publish unreleased work, confidential information, or material
  under third-party licences that do not allow portfolio use (some stock and
  font licences do not).
- Collaborators may show released work with credit to the studio and without
  naming the client unless the client is publicly named (collaborator
  agreement, clause 6).

## Outputs

The SOW rights section; the handover asset register listing every
third-party asset, licence, licensee and AI disclosure; publication clearance
records in the vault.

## Quality gate

- [ ] SOW states which deliverables are assigned and which licensed.
- [ ] Every contributor has a signed collaborator agreement on file.
- [ ] Every font, stock item, plugin and dependency is in the asset register with licence and licensee.
- [ ] No AI-generated material in marks or exclusive elements.
- [ ] AI use disclosed in the handover.
- [ ] No copyleft code without written client agreement.
- [ ] Publication cleared under [D-0021](../../studio/decisions/0021-client-work-public-anonymised.md) and MSA clause 7 (no confidential opt-out).

## Metrics

Handover asset registers complete at L7 (target: every project); licence
issues found after handover (target: none).

## Failure modes

- A logo built on a stock icon or a font without logo rights.
- Assigning work a collaborator never assigned to the studio.
- Shipping web fonts under a desktop licence.
- Promising exclusive ownership of AI-generated imagery.
- Publishing a case study before the client launches.
