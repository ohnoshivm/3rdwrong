# Indian tax basics for a design and web studio

**Verify with your chartered accountant before acting.** This is an
orientation map, not tax advice. Tax law, rates, thresholds, forms and section
numbers change; every point below names an official source to check the
current position. Where this page and an official source disagree, the
official source wins.

**Proposed.** Reviewed against official-source names on 2026-10-23; figures
are deliberately omitted where they change often.

## Purpose

Know which tax questions arise when a studio in India bills Indian and
foreign clients, so the right ones reach the chartered accountant early and
invoices are correct first time.

## When it applies

Before the first invoice, at every new client country or client type, at each
monthly finance review, and whenever the studio's turnover approaches a
threshold.

## Roles

Operations — finance-legal maintains this page and the CA relationship. The
**chartered accountant** gives the advice. The **founder** signs returns and
decides.

## Inputs

Turnover to date, client list by country and GST registration (vault),
invoices, bank and payment-platform statements.

## Steps

### 1. GST registration

- **Documented** — Registration is required once aggregate turnover in a
  financial year exceeds the threshold in section 22 of the Central Goods
  and Services Tax Act, 2017; the threshold is lower in special category
  states. Read the current figure on the official portals below.
  Aggregate turnover includes exports and exempt supplies. Some suppliers
  must register regardless of turnover (section 24). Sources:
  [CBIC GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/), [GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in/).
- **Interpretation** — Voluntary registration below the threshold may suit a
  studio with Indian business clients (they can claim input tax credit) or
  exports under LUT. Ask the CA before registering: it brings monthly or
  quarterly return obligations.

### 2. SAC codes and rates

- Services are classified by Services Accounting Code (SAC) under heading
  9983 (other professional, technical and business services) and related
  headings. Candidate codes the CA should confirm per deliverable:

  | Work | Candidate SAC to confirm |
  | --- | --- |
  | Identity, graphic and specialty design | 998391 (specialty design services) |
  | Original design artwork | 998392 (design originals) |
  | Website and app design and development | 998314 (IT design and development services) |
  | Advertising services | 998361 |

- The rate is set by the services rate notification on
  [CBIC GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/). Check it for each SAC; do not copy a
  rate from a blog or an older invoice.
- Inside the same state, invoice CGST + SGST; to another state, IGST. Place
  of supply rules for services decide which applies (Integrated Goods and
  Services Tax Act, 2017, sections 12–13).

### 3. Export of services under LUT

- **Documented** — A supply of services is an export when the supplier is in
  India, the recipient is outside India, the place of supply is outside
  India, payment is received in convertible foreign exchange (or in Indian
  rupees where the RBI permits), and the two are not establishments of the
  same person (IGST Act, section 2(6)). Exports are zero-rated (section 16).
- A registered studio can export without paying IGST by filing a Letter of
  Undertaking (LUT) in form GST RFD-11 on the [GST portal](https://www.gst.gov.in/)
  for each financial year, before making such exports. Otherwise it pays IGST
  and claims a refund.
- The export invoice must carry the export endorsement (for supplies under
  LUT: "Supply meant for export under Letter of Undertaking without payment
  of integrated tax") and the LUT reference.

### 4. Foreign remittance documentation

- Receive foreign payments through a bank or an authorised payment platform
  and keep, for each receipt: the inward remittance advice or Foreign
  Inward Remittance Advice (FIRA), the purpose code the bank used, and the
  matching invoice. Check that the purpose code describes the service.
- Electronic bank realisation certificates (e-BRC) are issued through the
  [DGFT](https://www.dgft.gov.in/) system for export realisation.
- **Documented** — Export proceeds must be realised and repatriated within
  the period set by the RBI's export regulations. The RBI revised these
  regulations and the realisation period more than once in 2025–26; read the
  current notification on [rbi.org.in](https://www.rbi.org.in/) before
  agreeing long payment terms with a foreign client.
- Ask the CA whether any software deliverable needs a SOFTEX filing.

### 5. TDS deducted by Indian clients

- **Documented** — Indian business clients may deduct tax at source (TDS) from
  professional or technical fees and from contract payments, at rates and
  above thresholds set by the income-tax law. The Income-tax Act, 2025
  replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from 1 April 2026; provisions previously
  known by section numbers such as 194J (fees for professional or technical
  services) and 194C (contracts, which include advertising) now sit under
  the new Act's TDS section. Check the current section, rate and threshold on
  [incometaxindia.gov.in](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/).
- Which provision applies to design or web work can be disputed; agree it
  with the client before the first invoice so receipts reconcile.
- TDS is a prepayment of the studio's income tax, not a loss. Reconcile it
  each month against the Annual Information Statement / Form 26AS and collect
  the TDS certificate each quarter.

### 6. Income tax and other obligations

- Advance tax is due in instalments during the year when liability exceeds
  the statutory limit; dates on [incometaxindia.gov.in](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/).
- Ask the CA whether presumptive taxation for professionals applies to the
  studio's work and structure.
- Professional tax is levied by some states; check the state's rules.
- If the studio pays collaborators, it may itself have to deduct TDS and file
  TDS returns.

### Invoice must-haves

Rule 46 of the Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017 lists the
particulars of a tax invoice ([CBIC GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/)). For a
services studio, check each invoice has:

- supplier name, address and GSTIN;
- a consecutive serial number unique for the financial year, of at most
  sixteen characters (letters, digits, hyphen or slash);
- date of issue;
- recipient name and address, and GSTIN if registered;
- for unregistered recipients above the rule's value limit, the delivery
  address and state;
- SAC code and description of the service;
- taxable value, net of any discount;
- tax rate and amount for each of CGST, SGST/UTGST or IGST;
- place of supply with state name, for inter-state supplies;
- whether tax is payable on reverse charge;
- signature or digital signature of the supplier or authorised person;
- for exports: the export endorsement, recipient country, currency and the
  LUT reference.

Issue a service invoice within the period the rules allow after supplying the
service (Rule 47). An advance such as a deposit may need a receipt voucher and
may create a tax liability when received; ask the CA how to treat deposits.

## Outputs

A CA-confirmed tax setup recorded in the vault: registration status, SAC per
service line, LUT status, TDS treatment per client type, return calendar.

## Quality gate

- [ ] CA has confirmed registration status, SAC codes and rates.
- [ ] LUT filed for the current financial year before any zero-rated export.
- [ ] Invoice template checked against Rule 46 by the CA.
- [ ] Foreign receipts matched to FIRA or e-BRC and invoice.
- [ ] TDS reconciled monthly; certificates collected quarterly.
- [ ] Return and advance-tax dates in the operating-rhythm calendar.

## Metrics

Returns filed on time (target: all), TDS certificates outstanding, foreign
receipts without remittance documents (target: none).

## Failure modes

- Crossing the registration threshold without noticing.
- Exporting before filing the LUT, then owing IGST.
- Missing TDS certificates, so credit is not claimed.
- Accepting foreign payment terms longer than the RBI allows.
- Copying a SAC code or rate from someone else's invoice.
