## What it does

- Maps AI autonomy and potential harm to suitable human-oversight patterns.
- Selects relevant Governors such as plans, verification, citations, controls, branches and cost estimates.
- Explains concrete interaction patterns and their friction trade-offs.
- Recommends only the patterns relevant to the product’s stakes.

## When to use

- When the approved scope specifically requires designing or reviewing human oversight, transparency, consent, and trust signals in AI experiences. In practice: during experience design or validation, against the named user task and flow.

## When not to use

- Do not use it to certify accessibility or production readiness; use `skill:accessibility` for design-level access risks and `skill:reviewing-interface-quality` for rendered interface quality.

## How to run it

1. Confirm the exact surface, task, scope and evidence before judging.
2. Apply only the source tool’s relevant method and record concrete observations.
3. Prioritise findings by user consequence and pair each with a usable next action.
4. State evidence gaps and route any specialist checks to the relevant owner.
