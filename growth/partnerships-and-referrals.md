# Partnerships and referrals

**Proposed.** Referral asks at L9, agency partnerships (including specialist
creative-engineering support for other agencies) and community participation.

## Purpose

Grow through trust that already exists: clients who were well served,
agencies that need a capability the studio has, and communities where the
studio contributes before it asks.

## When it applies

- **Referrals:** at L9 close-out and when a client says something positive.
- **Agency partnerships:** when an agency or studio asks for help, or the
  studio identifies an agency whose clients need a capability it lacks.
- **Communities:** continuously, at a pace that does not eat craft time.

## Roles

| Role | Department | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Referrals and partners | Outbound — partnerships-referrals | Asks, partner pipeline, community plan |
| Partner delivery terms | Sales — proposals-pitches; Operations — finance-legal | Scope, credit, confidentiality and contract terms (Business engine) |
| **Founder** | Sign-off | Approves every partnership and its credit terms |

## Inputs

Closed projects and their feedback; the pipeline; the studio's capabilities;
the [initial ICP](../studio/initial-icp.md)'s partner-agency prospect category.

## Steps

### Referral asks (L9)

1. Ask only after the client has accepted the work and said it went well.
2. Make it specific and easy: "Is there one founder you know facing a launch
   or repositioning who might find a conversation useful? I'm happy to send a
   short note you can forward."
3. Ask once. Thank them whatever the answer. No referral fees or incentives
   unless the founder approves a disclosed arrangement.
4. Record a referral as an L0 lead with source `referral-client` and the
   referrer in the vault; tell the referrer what happened, without
   confidential details.

### Agency partnerships

The studio can serve agencies in two ways:

| Model | What the studio provides | Key terms to agree |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specialist creative-engineering support | WebGL/3D, motion, creative front-end or performance work inside the agency's project | Scope and acceptance owner; who talks to the end client; credit and portfolio rights; confidentiality; non-solicitation both ways |
| Identity capability | Identity-system work for an agency without that discipline | As above, plus who owns the strategy and the client relationship |

Steps:

1. **Qualify the partner** like a client ([qualification](sales/qualification.md)):
   quality of their work, how they treat collaborators, payment reliability,
   fit with the studio's standards.
2. **Agree terms in writing before work starts** (Business engine contracts):
   white-label or credited, portfolio rights, end-client contact rules.
3. **Start with one bounded project**, then review the relationship.
4. **Track** partner leads with source `partner-agency`.

**Hypothesis:** credited specialist work for strong agencies can build proof
faster than direct clients while the portfolio is thin; test it and compare
margin and portfolio value (vault) against direct work.

### Communities

- Contribute first: answer questions, share build notes, give talks or
  critiques in design and creative-technology communities the ICP or peers
  actually use.
- Choose at most two communities at a time and show up consistently.
- Never pitch in community spaces unless invited; enquiries go through
  [inbound handling](content/inbound.md).

## Outputs

Referral and partner leads in the vault pipeline; partner agreements (vault);
a short list of active communities in the monthly content log.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Referral asked once, at the right moment, and recorded.
- [ ] Partner qualified and terms signed before work.
- [ ] Credit and confidentiality agreed explicitly.
- [ ] Community participation adds value before asking for anything.

## Metrics

Referral asks made and referrals received; partner leads and wins; share of
won work by source ([growth metrics](metrics.md)).

## Failure modes

- Asking for referrals from an unhappy or unfinished project.
- White-label work that cannot be shown and earns too little to justify it.
- Partner owns the client relationship and the studio absorbs the risk.
- Treating communities as an ad channel.
