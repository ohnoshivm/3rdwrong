# Qualification

**Proposed.** Lifecycle stage **L1 Qualify**: decide whether to pursue an
opportunity, with an ICP-fit scorecard, hard disqualifiers and red flags.

## Purpose

Spend discovery time only on opportunities where the studio can do excellent
work, the buyer can decide and pay, and the work moves the studio towards its
positioning. Decline the rest early and kindly.

## When it applies

Every L0 lead that is a plausible business need. Re-run it when something
material changes (budget, decision-maker, scope, timing).

## Roles

| Role | Department | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualifier | Sales — discovery-qualification | Runs the qualification call or exchange, scores the opportunity |
| Craft check | Design or Engineering lead | Flags feasibility concerns for unusual requests (for example real-time 3D) |
| **Founder** | Sign-off | Decides pursue, not yet or decline |

## Inputs

The L0 pipeline record; the enquiry; public information about the business
(website, social presence, press); a 15–20 minute qualification call or a
short written exchange if a call is not possible.

## Steps

1. **Prepare (15 minutes).** Read their site and recent public activity. Note
   one observation and one question. Do not diagnose their brand in writing.
2. **Qualification conversation (15–20 minutes).** Ask about the trigger
   ("What made this a priority now?"), what success would look like, who else
   decides, timing, whether they have a budget range in mind, and — for
   digital work — who owns content and maintenance afterwards. Listen more
   than you pitch. Close with an honest next step.
3. **Score** the opportunity with the scorecard below. Score from evidence
   heard, not hope; mark anything unknown as 0 and note it as a discovery
   question.
4. **Check disqualifiers.** Any one present means decline (or "not yet" when
   it could change), regardless of score.
5. **Founder decision.** Pursue → book discovery ([L2](discovery-call.md)).
   Not yet → record the condition and a check-back date. Decline → send a
   short, respectful note; refer elsewhere when you can.
6. **Record** the score, decision and reason in the vault pipeline record.

### ICP-fit scorecard

Aligned to the [initial ICP](../../studio/initial-icp.md) (a hypothesis) and
to D-0018's two pillars. Score each criterion 0–3, multiply by its weight and
sum; the maximum is 300.

| Criterion | Weight | 0 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Genuine problem the studio solves | 20 | No clear problem, or only production capacity wanted | Identity or digital-experience problem stated by the buyer, with consequences |
| Decision-maker access | 15 | No contact with whoever decides | Founder, co-founder or brand lead with creative authority is in the conversation |
| Buying trigger and timing | 15 | No reason to act; no date | ICP trigger (launch, repositioning, new market, professionalising the identity) with a realistic date |
| Budget band fit | 15 | Budget clearly below the studio's floor (vault) | Budget range named and within the band for the likely offer |
| Offer fit | 10 | Outside both pillars | Clearly an Identity System, a Digital Experience grounded in an identity, or the Brand Direction Sprint |
| Openness to original thinking and discovery | 10 | Wants a copy of a competitor or a trend | Wants an original idea and will take part in discovery and reviews |
| Readiness | 5 | No content, no owner, no inputs | Inputs, content owner and (for digital) a maintenance owner identified |
| Proof and publication potential | 5 | Publication impossible and work adds nothing to the portfolio | Likely to permit anonymised publication, or strengthens a target portfolio area |
| Relationship and conduct | 5 | Disrespectful, evasive or slow without reason | Clear, responsive and respectful |

**Starting thresholds (Hypothesis):** 210 or more → pursue; 150–209 → founder
judgement, usually "pursue with a named risk" or "not yet"; below 150 →
decline or "not yet". Recalibrate after the first ten decided opportunities
by comparing scores with wins, losses and project health; record the change
here through review.

### Disqualifiers (decline or "not yet")

- Asks for free speculative creative work or an unpaid pitch with concepts.
- Wants cheap logo production, unlimited revisions or a guaranteed outcome
  (sales lift, an award) the studio cannot honestly promise.
- Wants a copy of another brand's identity or site.
- Budget firmly below the studio's floor for any useful scope, with no
  smaller option (such as the [Brand Direction Sprint](../../studio/brand-direction-sprint.md))
  that would genuinely help.
- A deadline that cannot be met without cutting discovery, critique or QA.
- Conflict of interest with a current client the studio has agreed not to
  compete with.
- Work or claims the founder will not put the studio's name to; the list of
  excluded categories is a founder decision.
- Asks the studio to misrepresent its experience, results or use of AI.

### Red flags (discuss, do not auto-decline)

- Several previous agencies "didn't get it" with no clear reason.
- Refuses to discuss any budget range after a respectful second ask.
- Decision by committee with no named owner.
- Procurement process that asks for concepts before selection.
- Pressure to start before scope or deposit (L4).
- Digital work with no content owner or maintenance owner.

## Outputs

A qualification note in the vault pipeline record: score by criterion,
disqualifier check, decision, reason and next action. Anonymised counts may
appear in public metrics.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Every criterion scored from evidence; unknowns marked 0 and listed as
      discovery questions.
- [ ] Disqualifiers checked explicitly.
- [ ] Founder decision recorded with a reason.
- [ ] Declines sent within two working days, politely, with a referral where
      possible.
- [ ] No prospect identity in a public file.

## Metrics

Lead-to-qualified rate by source; share of pursued opportunities later won;
score distribution of wins against losses (to recalibrate the thresholds).
See [growth metrics](../metrics.md).

## Failure modes

- Pursuing everything because the pipeline is thin; it fills the calendar
  with poor-fit discovery calls.
- Scoring optimistically; unknowns treated as "probably fine".
- Qualifying out good-fit clients because they ask hard questions about
  budget or proof — those are buyers doing their job.
- Treating the ICP as proven; it is a hypothesis under D-0004.
