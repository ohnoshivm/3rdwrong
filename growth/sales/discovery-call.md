# Discovery call

**Proposed.** Lifecycle stage **L2 Discover & scope**: understand the problem
well enough to scope it, then hand a complete record to the [Business engine](../../business/README.md)
for L3 Propose.

## Purpose

Leave the buyer feeling understood and the studio able to write a proposal
the buyer recognises in its first paragraph. Discovery is a working session,
not a sales script: the aim is shared clarity, including the clarity that the
studio is not the right fit.

## When it applies

After a founder "pursue" decision at [L1](qualification.md). One call is
usual; complex digital work may need a second, technical session.

## Roles

| Role | Department | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead | Sales — discovery-qualification (usually the founder) | Runs the call, writes the notes and summary |
| Strategy | Strategy — positioning | Problem framing; joins or reviews notes |
| Feasibility | Design and Engineering leads | Confirm the scope is deliverable at the studio's standard |
| **Founder** | Sign-off | Confirms the scope is deliverable before L3 |

## Inputs

The qualification note and its open questions; the buyer's site, materials
and anything they share before the call; the relevant offer page (Identity
System, Digital Experience or [Brand Direction Sprint](../../studio/brand-direction-sprint.md)).

## Steps

1. **Prepare (45 minutes).** Render the `discovery-notes` bundle into the
   vault. Review their public presence, two or three competitors and the
   qualification gaps. Write three hypotheses about the problem, labelled
   **Hypothesis**, to test — not to present.
2. **Run the call (60 minutes)** with the agenda below. Take notes in the
   buyer's words. Ask permission before recording; store any recording only
   in the vault.
3. **Book the next step before leaving:** the written summary date and,
   if the fit is good, the proposal presentation (the [Business engine](../../business/README.md)
   presents live within about five working days).
4. **Send the written summary within one working day:** problem, outcomes,
   scope boundary (in, out, assumptions), client inputs and dates, decision
   process and date, and open questions. Ask the buyer to confirm or correct it.
5. **Feasibility check.** Design and Engineering leads confirm the scope is
   deliverable at the studio's standard within the timing discussed.
6. **Hand off to L3** with the pack below once the buyer confirms the summary.

### Agenda (60 minutes)

| Minutes | Segment | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Context and agenda | Agree the purpose: understand, then decide together whether and how to work |
| 5–25 | The business and the problem | Trigger, audience, competition, what is not working, consequences |
| 25–40 | Ambition and success | What good looks like in a year; how they will judge the work |
| 40–50 | Practicalities | Decision process, timing, budget band, inputs, constraints, risks |
| 50–60 | Next steps | Play back what you heard; agree the summary and the presentation date |

### Question bank

Pick, do not recite. Follow the buyer's answers.

#### All offers

- What made this a priority now? What happens if you do nothing this year?
- Who are you for, and who are you not for?
- Who do customers compare you with? What do you want them to notice first?
- What has already been tried? What did you learn from it?
- How will you judge whether this worked, six and twelve months from now?

#### Identity System

- Where does the current identity fail in use — packaging, social, retail,
  digital, partners?
- What must stay recognisable? What are you ready to let go of?
- Who will apply the identity day to day after handover?
- Which applications matter most in the first six months?
- Is there a naming, verbal or architecture question inside this?

#### Digital Experience

- Is the identity settled, or is it part of this work? (If not settled, the
  identity question comes first: the Digital Experience offer does not hide a
  rebrand inside a website.)
- What must a visitor understand or do? What would make them return or share?
- What content exists, and who writes and owns it?
- What platforms, CMS, integrations and languages are required?
- Who maintains the site after launch, and with what skills?
- Is interactive or 3D work wanted for a reason a visitor would feel, or as
  decoration? What devices and connections must it work on?
- Are there accessibility, performance or hosting requirements?

### Listening for budget, decision process, timing and risk

- **Budget band.** Ask plainly and once: "Do you have a range in mind for
  this? It helps me suggest the right scope rather than the biggest one."
  If they have none, describe how the studio prices (options by scope) and
  ask which end feels closer. Record the band in the vault only.
- **Decision process.** "Who else will want a say, and how will you decide
  between options?" Note the decider, influencers, approvers and the date.
- **Timing.** Separate the buyer's real constraint (a launch, a fundraise, a
  season) from preference. Never invent urgency; state the studio's real
  capacity window when relevant.
- **Risk.** Listen for missing content, absent owners, fixed launch dates
  with no slack, committee approvals, conflicting stakeholder goals, and
  technical unknowns. Record each with a mitigation or a question.

### Hand-off to L3: what Business needs

The [Business engine](../../business/README.md)'s proposals method expects these before it drafts:

- [ ] Problem statement in the client's words, and the decision question.
- [ ] Three outcomes with observable signals.
- [ ] Offer and likely scope options; in, out, assumptions.
- [ ] Client inputs and their dates; content and maintenance owners.
- [ ] Decision-maker, influencers, decision process and decision date.
- [ ] Budget band and constraints (vault only).
- [ ] Timing: real constraint, start window, presentation date booked.
- [ ] Risks and mitigations; feasibility confirmed by Design and Engineering.
- [ ] Publication preference (anonymised case study possible or not), for
      the contract's clearance clause.
- [ ] Written summary confirmed by the client.

## Outputs

Discovery notes and the confirmed summary in the vault
(`discovery-notes` bundle, `{vault}/{codename}/private-notes/`); pipeline
stage updated; hand-off pack complete.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Summary sent within one working day and confirmed by the client.
- [ ] Every hand-off item answered or explicitly marked unknown with an owner.
- [ ] Feasibility confirmed by the craft leads.
- [ ] No pressure: next steps agreed, not imposed.
- [ ] Nothing identifying in the repository.

## Metrics

Discovery-to-presentation days (Business scorecard); share of discovery calls
that reach a proposal; share of proposals whose problem statement the client
accepted without correction.

## Failure modes

- Pitching in discovery — presenting ideas before the problem is understood.
- Giving away the strategy for free in the call or the summary.
- Leaving without a decision process or date.
- Proposing digital work on top of an unresolved identity.
- Accepting a scope the craft leads have not checked.
