# Outbound system

**Proposed.** Lifecycle stage **L0 Lead** from the studio's side: choosing a
small number of right-fit accounts, researching them properly and starting a
genuine conversation. No spray-and-pray.

## Purpose

Start conversations with businesses the studio is well placed to help, at a
moment when the help is relevant, in a way that would feel welcome if the
roles were reversed.

## When it applies

All proactive contact with people who have not asked to hear from the studio:
email, LinkedIn messages and Instagram DMs. Read
[compliance basics](compliance.md) before the first send to any new country.

## Roles

| Role | Department | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Researcher | Outbound — prospecting-research | Builds and maintains the target-account list; researches each account |
| Writer | Outbound — outreach-sequences | Writes each personalised message and the sequence |
| Reply triage | Outbound → Sales | Classifies replies; positive replies become L0 leads |
| **Founder** | Sign-off | Approves the account list each month and every first message until the quality bar is proven |

## Inputs

The [initial ICP](../../studio/initial-icp.md) and its discovery focus (D-0010:
beauty and personal care first); the studio's cleared proof; public sources
about each account.

## Steps

1. **Select target accounts from the ICP** (monthly).
2. **Research** each account with the checklist (per account, before writing).
3. **Write a personalised sequence** with the `outreach-sequence` bundle,
   rendered into the vault.
4. **Send** within the volume bar; log each touch in the vault.
5. **Handle replies** within one working day.
6. **Review** the month: reply rate, positive replies, qualified conversations,
   opt-outs; adjust the list, not the honesty.

### 1. Target-account selection

Build the list in the vault's `growth/accounts.yaml`. Every account needs:

- a fit with the ICP: founder-led, operating, premium or aspirational, with
  evidence of demand (D-0004 hypothesis);
- a **visible trigger** from public evidence: a launch, new category, funding
  announcement, retail expansion, new market, or a hire in brand or marketing;
- a reason this studio specifically could help (an offer pillar and a relevant
  piece of proof or method);
- a reachable decision-maker (founder, co-founder, brand or marketing lead).

No trigger, no outreach: park the account and watch it.

### 2. Research checklist (20–30 minutes per account)

- [ ] What they sell, to whom, at what positioning; channels (D2C, retail, marketplaces).
- [ ] The trigger, with its public source and access date.
- [ ] How the brand currently appears across site, packaging, social and
      retail — observations only, no public criticism.
- [ ] The likely decision-maker and their role; how they prefer to be
      contacted, if stated.
- [ ] One genuine, specific reason for contacting them now.
- [ ] One relevant piece of studio proof or method (cleared or self-initiated
      and labelled).
- [ ] Country of the recipient and the rules that apply ([compliance](compliance.md)).
- [ ] Any reason not to contact them (current agency relationship announced,
      conflict, previous opt-out).

Store research links and notes in the vault, never in the repository.

### 3. Personalised outreach: principles

- Write to one person about their business. If the message could be sent to
  another company by changing the name, rewrite it.
- Lead with the observation and the reason now, not the studio's credentials.
- Offer value without unsolicited redesigns: a relevant essay, a principle, a
  question. Never send speculative rebrands or public teardowns.
- One clear, low-effort ask (a 20-minute conversation, or "is this on your
  radar this year?"), or none.
- Identify the studio honestly; say how you found them; make opting out easy.
- No fake familiarity, false "Re:" subjects, invented mutual connections or
  fabricated scarcity.

### Sequence structures

| Channel | Touches | Spacing | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email | Up to 3 | Day 0, day 5–7, day 14–21 | 1: observation, reason now, one proof, soft ask. 2: a useful resource relevant to their trigger. 3: brief close-the-loop, easy opt-out |
| LinkedIn | Connection note, then up to 2 messages | Day 0, after acceptance, +7–10 days | Note: one line, why connecting. Message 1: as email 1, shorter. Message 2: close the loop |
| Instagram DM | 1, at most 2 | Day 0, +10–14 days | Only for brands that use DMs for business; short, warm, specific; offer to move to email |

Use one primary channel per person; a second channel only if the first is
silent and only once. Stop at the first reply of any kind and switch to
[reply handling](#5-reply-handling).

### Example messages (fictional prospect only)

Stillsoil Atelier is a fictional, founder-led ceramics and home-fragrance
brand. Bracketed fields are placeholders.

#### Email 1

> Subject: Stillsoil's move into home fragrance
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> I saw Stillsoil announce the new home-fragrance line in [source, month].
> Moving from ceramics into scent changes what the brand has to carry: a

>
> I run 3RDWRONG, a design-engineering studio that builds identity systems
> meant to hold up across that kind of expansion. This short piece on how we
> test an identity at small sizes may be useful whatever you decide: [link].
>
> If rethinking the identity for the new line is on your list this year, I'd
> be glad to compare notes for 20 minutes. If not, no reply needed.
>
> [Name], 3RDWRONG
> [studio postal address] · Reply "no thanks" and I won't write again.

#### Email 2 (day 6)

> Hi [first name], one more thought on the fragrance launch: [one specific,
> useful observation or resource]. Happy to talk if helpful. [Name]

#### Email 3 (day 18)

> Hi [first name], I'll leave this here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If
> the identity question comes up later, I'd be glad to hear from you. [Name]

#### LinkedIn connection note

> Hi [first name], I follow Stillsoil's work in ceramics and saw the fragrance
> launch. I run a small identity and digital studio and would like to connect.

### 4. Volume and quality bar

- **Quality first:** every message passes the research checklist and the
  "could this go to another company?" test. The founder reviews first
  messages until ten consecutive sequences pass without edits.
- **Volume:** 20 personalised first messages a week ([D-0024](../../studio/decisions/0024-growth-defaults.md)), each
  researched and written by hand; never batch-send or automate first
  messages. The earlier hypothesis was 5–10 accounts a week; if research
  quality slips, send fewer rather than weaker messages and flag the capacity
  risk for review.
- **Stop signals:** opt-out rate rising, replies calling the message
  irrelevant, or spam complaints — pause and review the list and messages.

### 5. Reply handling

| Reply | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Interested | Thank them; propose times; create the L0 lead (source `outbound`) |
| Not now | Ask, once, if a later date suits; set a "not yet" check-back; no further sequence |
| Wrong person / referral | Thank them; contact the named person only if introduced or clearly appropriate |
| Objection or question | Answer honestly and briefly; offer a call only if useful |
| No / unsubscribe / annoyed | Apologise briefly if appropriate, confirm they will not hear again, add to the vault suppression list the same day |
| Auto-reply | Pause and resume after the stated return date |

## Outputs

Account list, research notes, sequences and the suppression list in the
vault; anonymised monthly counts in the business review.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Account has an ICP fit and a sourced trigger.
- [ ] Research checklist complete; compliance checked for the recipient's country.
- [ ] Message passes the personalisation test and principles above.
- [ ] Opt-out honoured the same day; suppression list checked before every send.
- [ ] No prospect names or contact details in the repository.

## Metrics

Accounts researched, messages sent, reply rate, positive-reply rate,
qualified conversations from outbound, opt-out rate
([growth metrics](../metrics.md)).

## Failure modes

- Buying lists or scraping contacts without a lawful basis.
- Template messages with a name swapped in.
- Unsolicited redesigns or criticism of a prospect's current brand.
- Continuing a sequence after any reply or an opt-out.
- Letting outbound volume eat craft time.
