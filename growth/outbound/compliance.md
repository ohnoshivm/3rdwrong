# Outreach compliance basics

> **Not legal advice — confirm with a lawyer for your situation.** This page
> summarises official sources as read on 2026-10-23 so the studio asks the
> right questions. Laws, rules and regulator guidance change; read the source,
> and get advice before contacting people in a new country at any volume.

## Purpose

Keep studio outreach lawful, respectful and easy to refuse, and record why
each contact was made.

## When it applies

Before outreach to anyone who has not asked to hear from the studio, and
whenever the studio stores data about prospects in the vault.

## Roles

| Role | Department | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outbound lead | Outbound — outreach-sequences | Applies this page to every sequence |
| Data owner | Operations — finance-legal | Keeps the vault's suppression list, retention and deletion |
| **Founder** | Sign-off | Accountable; decides when to take legal advice |

## Inputs

The recipient's country and whether they are contacted as an individual or a
company; the source of their contact details; the vault suppression list.

## Steps

1. Identify where the recipient is and which rules below apply.
2. Record, in the vault, where the contact details came from and why the
   studio is contacting this person (the basis).
3. Write the message with the common requirements below.
4. Check the suppression list before every send; honour opt-outs the same day.
5. Keep only what the outreach needs; delete prospect data the studio no
   longer needs, at least at each quarterly review.

### Common requirements (apply everywhere)

- Identify the studio and the sender truthfully; no misleading subject lines
  or headers.
- Say how you found them and why you are writing.
- Give a simple way to opt out in every message, and honour it promptly and
  permanently (suppression list in the vault).
- Collect the minimum: name, role, business, a work contact, the research
  link and the outreach log. No sensitive data.
- Do not buy contact lists or use scraping tools against a platform's terms.
- Respect platform rules: LinkedIn's
  [User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) restricts
  automated activity and spam; Instagram's
  [Terms of Use](https://help.instagram.com/581066165581870) apply to DMs.

### India — Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023

- **Source:** the Act and the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025, on
  the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology's
  [data protection framework page](https://www.meity.gov.in/data-protection-framework).
- The Act governs digital personal data, including a person's work email or
  phone number. Processing needs consent or one of the Act's specified
  "legitimate uses"; consent must be free, specific, informed and withdrawable,
  with a notice explaining the purpose.
- The Act excludes personal data that the person made publicly available
  themselves; how far that covers outreach using a published business contact
  is a question for a lawyer.
- The Rules were notified in November 2025 with obligations commencing in
  phases; check the official page for what is in force on the date you act.
- Telemarketing calls and SMS fall under separate TRAI rules; the studio does
  not cold-call or SMS.

### European Union — GDPR and ePrivacy

- **Sources:** [GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
  and the [ePrivacy Directive 2002/58/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2002/58/oj),
  as implemented in each member state.
- A business contact's name and work email are personal data. Outreach needs a
  lawful basis (Article 6); legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) requires a
  documented balancing of the studio's interest against the person's.
- When data was not collected from the person, they must be told who the
  studio is, the purpose, the source and their rights, at the latest at the
  first communication (Article 14).
- People have an absolute right to object to direct marketing (Article 21);
  stop immediately.
- Rules on unsolicited email to individuals and to companies differ by member
  state under ePrivacy; check the recipient's country.

### United Kingdom — UK GDPR and PECR

- **Source:** the Information Commissioner's Office guidance on
  [electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/).
- Under PECR, marketing email to **corporate subscribers** (companies, LLPs,
  some partnerships, government bodies) does not need prior consent, but the
  studio must identify itself and give a valid opt-out; keep a "do not email"
  list.
- **Individual subscribers** — sole traders and some partnerships — need
  specific consent or the soft opt-in, which a cold prospect does not have.
  Many founder-led brands are sole traders: check before emailing.
- UK GDPR still applies to the personal data of named employees.
- The ICO notes its guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access)
  Act; read the current version.

### United States — CAN-SPAM Act

- **Source:** the Federal Trade Commission's
  [CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide for business](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business).
- Applies to commercial email, including business-to-business email.
- No false or misleading header information or subject lines; identify the
  message as an advertisement where required; include a valid physical postal
  address; give a clear opt-out and honour it within ten business days; the
  studio remains responsible for anyone sending on its behalf.
- State laws may add requirements.

## Outputs

A basis and source recorded per contact in the vault; the suppression list;
quarterly deletion of data no longer needed.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Recipient's country identified and the rules above checked.
- [ ] Source and basis recorded in the vault.
- [ ] Message identifies the studio, explains the contact and offers an opt-out.
- [ ] Suppression list checked; opt-outs honoured the same day.
- [ ] Legal advice taken before scaling outreach into a new jurisdiction.

## Metrics

Opt-out rate; time to honour opt-outs; prospect records past their retention
review ([growth metrics](../metrics.md)).

## Failure modes

- Treating "B2B" as an exemption everywhere; it is not.
- Emailing UK or EU sole traders without consent.
- Keeping prospect data indefinitely.
- Using automation that breaches platform terms.
