## Working audience and trigger

Within the fiction, intended buyers are people considering Stillsoil Atelier's
cleansing bars and body oil; specific segments remain **Unknown**. The trigger is
a fictional request for a line sheet and more consistent identity. Any broader
claim about Indian personal-care buyers is **Unknown** and would be a
**Hypothesis** until researched with real sources and participants.

## Unknowns and risks

- **Unknown:** what “premium” means to any real buyer; the brief's label is not market evidence.
- **Unknown:** whether the fictional retailer will stock the products.
- **Unknown:** actual label, line-sheet and mobile behaviour because assets were not supplied.
- **Unknown:** required languages, scripts, legal product text and vendor production tolerances.
- **Hypothesis:** a consistent name and variant hierarchy may improve wayfinding; test on representative applications and with intended users.

## Questions for diagnosis and strategy

1. Which information must be noticed first on the smallest in-scope application?
2. What does “considered, not clinical” mean in product and communication decisions, beyond a visual mood?
3. Which product facts and scripts must remain readable on label, line sheet and mobile shop?
4. Is the retailer request a genuine buying trigger or exploratory feedback?
5. What evidence would show that the identity is easier to recognise or use? Do not infer this from the fictional notes.

## Claims not to make

- Do not claim that Indian premium personal-care buyers prefer minimal or tactile design.
- Do not claim product efficacy, clinical safety, market demand or retailer commitment.
- Do not claim improved sales, recall or recognition from these synthetic notes.
- Do not call four fictional notes representative research.
